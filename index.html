Method

Speed means time from listing to sale

The metric

Of all $100+ listings at least 7 days old, the share that sold within 7 days of going live. Times come from Poshmark's own listed and sold timestamps.

Excluded

Items sold under 1 hour after listing. These are mostly Posh Show or reserved sales, where a listing is created for a buyer who already agreed to buy.

Why not sales volume

Volume rewards brands with lots of supply. Chanel sells more pieces per day than anyone but has so many listings that each one waits longer.

What makes a $100+ item sell within a week

Ranked from the biggest help to the biggest hurt. Each bar compares items with that trait to their own brand's 7-day sell rate (17% on average). Right of the line sells faster, left sells slower.

Listings sell early or not at all

64% of eventual sales happen in the first 7 days. 71% of fast sales came after the seller cut the price, vs 84% of slower sales.

Extra effort didn't help

10+ photos, a video or a long description went with slower sales, even among similarly priced items. This is a correlation, not proof that photos hurt.

Season matters

Data is from October–November, which lifts coats and sweaters and hurts shorts. Expect these to flip in spring.

Data is from 9 brands: Dôen, Sézane, Orseund Iris, Paloma Wool, Realisation Par, Lemaire, Maison Margiela, Khaite and The Row. "Resellers" are sellers with 6+ of these listings in the data.

Limits

What this does not cover

  • Depop isn't measured. Its search hides sold items, so sold history can only be pulled shop by shop, and that sample was too thin (5 or fewer sales per brand).
  • Small samples: Realisation Par (42 listings), Buci (41) and Orseund Iris (67). Treat a gap of 1–2 points as noise.
  • Chanel, Prada and Gucci have too many listings to reach a 7-day window in this crawl.
  • Sale time is when Poshmark marked the item sold out. Offers accepted overnight can shift this by a few hours.
